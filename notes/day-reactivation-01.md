# Reactivation Day 1 — Return After Travel

Date: September 5, 2026

## How It Felt Coming Back

Returning to the terminal after more than a month away, the core Bash command line felt reassuringly familiar thanks to my prior background. However, several data manipulation tools felt rusty—particularly `uniq` and `sort`, which felt somewhat foreign in terms of truly understanding their mechanics rather than just recognizing their names. Commands like `grep` and `cut` also felt less solid than before.

What surprised me most was how much I actually retained. Going in, I felt a sense of inadequacy and self-doubt, assuming it would take a painful amount of time to re-learn everything after four weeks off. Surprisingly, reading through my daily notes brought the concepts back much faster than I anticipated. While I still need to restudy the underlying theory to ensure it sinks in deeply rather than remaining superficial knowledge, the refresher proved that the foundation is still there and came together quickly.

## What Reactivation Taught Me

Being away for over a month taught me firsthand what absence does to technical fluency: skills in coding, Linux, and bioinformatics deteriorate quickly without routine engagement. Bioinformatics cannot be learned through passive reading; it demands continuous, hands-on practice until the commands become second nature. It must become a daily lifestyle—even if only for 10 to 15 minutes a day to push a commit, manipulate a file, or inspect data. Showing up every day, no matter how brief the session, is the only way version control, file handling, and command-line intuition truly stick at your fingertips.
