# Week 2 Reflection — Git, GitHub, and the Journey So Far

**Date:** July 27, 2026
**Week:** 2 of 6 — Git and GitHub

---

## Looking Back

My bioinformatics learning journey has been an interesting one, though
tedious in places. Week 1 felt more like a refresher than a fresh
learning experience — I have had prior exposure to the Unix command
line shell, so revisiting those concepts came more naturally. It was
familiar ground, just sharpened.

Week 2 was a different story entirely. It has been the most
interesting — and I will not shy away from saying the most challenging
— part of the journey so far. I had encountered Git before, but I
never truly understood it. Having to study the concepts properly and
then practise them hands-on was a real challenge. But it was the kind
of challenge I enjoyed. The kind that makes you feel like something
is actually happening in your head.

---

## What I Still Feel Uncertain About

I will be honest: I still feel somewhat inadequate with Git and GitHub.
The commands, the committing, the branching, the pushing, the pulling —
I grasp the basic concepts, but I know that one week of study and
practice is not enough to make any of this second nature. I am going
to need to return to it repeatedly, use it as frequently as possible,
and keep drilling until it lives at my fingertips.

That is the plan. That has always been the plan.

---

## Why This Matters to Me

To be a phenomenal bioinformatician — the kind of independent
researcher I intend to become — the Bash command line and Git are
not optional extras. They are the foundation. When I eventually
collaborate with other researchers, whether in my specific field or
on a shared project, I need to be able to organise my repositories
properly, write clear and meaningful commit messages, and follow the
one-thing-per-commit rule so that every collaborator knows exactly
what I have done and what to expect. That is what professional,
reproducible science looks like in practice.

---

## Oh My Git! — An Unexpected Highlight

I started playing Oh My Git! as part of this week's tasks and I did
not intend to stop at just one session. I plan to keep playing it the
way other people play Candy Crush — regularly, casually, until the
flow of commits, branches, and merges becomes completely intuitive.
Seeing Git's history drawn as a living graph changed something for me.
It made the abstract feel concrete.

---

## Overall

Week 2 felt loaded. I know the weeks ahead are going to be even more
loaded — new languages, new tools, new concepts arriving faster than
I would like. But I am in for the ride.

The goal has not changed: to become a phenomenal bioinformatician,
to reach PhD-level research readiness, and to bring my absolute best
to whatever team or project I eventually find myself in.

Let's go.
